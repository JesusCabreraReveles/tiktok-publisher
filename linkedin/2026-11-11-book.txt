Real-time telemetry looks simple on a whiteboard: devices emit events, services react. In production it breaks in quieter ways: a producer changes a field and nobody notices, events arrive late or out of order, and state drifts until the dashboards stop telling the truth.

I wrote "Event-Driven Architecture for Real-Time Telemetry: A Field Playbook for Contracts, State, Time, and Resilient Event Flows" for the engineers who run these systems, not just design them.

It focuses on four things:
→ Contracts between producers and consumers
→ State that stays correct as events keep arriving
→ Time: events that arrive late or out of order
→ Event flows that keep working when parts of the system fail

It's a field playbook written by a practitioner: practical, and aimed at systems already in production.
