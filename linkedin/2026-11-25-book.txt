Most architecture decisions in distributed systems outlive the context that justified them. The people move on, the load changes, and a year later nobody can say why the system works the way it does, or whether the original reasons still hold.

I wrote "ADR Kit for Distributed Architectures: A Field Playbook for Recording, Reviewing, and Revisiting System Decisions" for the engineers and architects who have to live with those decisions.

It follows a decision through three stages:
→ Recording it while the context is fresh
→ Reviewing it before it ships
→ Revisiting it when the system changes

It's a field playbook written by a practitioner, aimed at real systems rather than whiteboard designs.
