Most outages don't get worse because of the original failure. They get worse because of our retries.

The pattern is always the same: the database slows down, calls start timing out, and every client retries immediately. Now a struggling service is getting more traffic than it had when it was healthy. The retries were meant to hide a blip. Instead they keep the system from recovering.

Retrying is fine. Retrying without rules is the problem. What I put in place before going to production:
→ Exponential backoff, so each attempt waits longer than the last.
→ Jitter, so thousands of clients don't come back at the same instant.
→ A retry budget, so retries can only ever be a small share of total traffic.
→ A circuit breaker in front of the dependency, so when it's sick we fail fast instead of piling on.

And retry only at one layer. If the client, the gateway and the service all retry, the attempts multiply.

None of this is new. It's just easy to skip when everything works in staging.

Where do your retries live today, and do you know how many attempts one failing request can turn into?
